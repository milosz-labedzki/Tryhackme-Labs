* `SOC` - Security Operations Center; a centralized organizational unit that continuously monitors, detects, analyzes, and responds to cybersecurity incidents.

* `Detection` - The process of identifying potential security threats, policy violations, or malicious activities within an IT infrastructure.

* `Response` - The operational actions taken to contain, mitigate, eradicate, and recover from security incidents.

* `People` - The skilled cybersecurity staff executing defense operations, triage, and decision-making.

* `Process` - The standardized protocols, workflows, playbooks, and policies governing how security operations are conducted.

* `Technology` - The hardware, software, telemetry sources, and automation tools supporting visibility and threat containment.

* `SOC Analyst (Level 1)` - Triage analyst responsible for monitoring alert queues, performing initial triage, filtering out false positives, and escalating true positives.

* `SOC Analyst (Level 2)` - Incident responder responsible for deep-dive investigation, threat containment, root-cause analysis, and executing remediation actions.

* `SOC Analyst (Level 3)` - Senior specialist handling complex incidents, proactive threat hunting, malware analysis, and forensic investigations.

* `Security Engineer` - Technical specialist who designs, deploys, integrates, and maintains SOC tools, infrastructure, and security platforms.

* `Detection Engineer` - Specialist focused on building, testing, and tuning detection rules, threat intelligence integrations, and behavioral analytics.

* `SOC Manager` - Operational leader managing SOC strategy, staffing, SLA compliance, performance metrics (MTTD/MTTR), and executive communication.

* `Alert Triade (5Ws)` - A systematic investigative methodology used to rapidly contextualize and validate security alerts.

* `What?` - The specific rule trigger, alert category, payload, or anomalous behavior observed.

* `When?` - The precise timestamp, sequence, and timeline of the malicious activity.

* `Where?` - The target asset, host, network segment, IP address, or cloud endpoint affected.

* `Who?` - The associated user account, process, service principal, or threat actor.

* `Why?` - The suspected root cause, exploited vulnerability, or operational context (e.g., policy violation vs. true malicious intent).

* `SIEM` - Security Information and Event Management; a platform that aggregates, correlates, and analyzes log data across an enterprise to detect threats and ensure compliance.

* `EDR` - Endpoint Detection and Response; an agent deployed on endpoints to record activity, detect advanced host threats, and enable remote containment.

* `Firewall` - A network security device that monitors and filters incoming and outgoing traffic based on defined access control policies.
