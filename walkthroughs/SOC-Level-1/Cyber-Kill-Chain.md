* `Reconnaissance` - The initial information-gathering phase where an attacker researches the target using active or passive reconnaissance techniques to identify weak points, software, and vulnerabilities.

* `Weaponization` - The process of combining a malicious exploit with a payload into a deliverable weapon, such as a malicious Office document or executable, tailored to the target system.

* `Delivery` - The transmission of the weaponized payload to the target host through mediums like spear-phishing emails, malicious URLs, or compromised USB drives.

* `Exploitation` - The triggering of the weaponized payload to execute malicious code on the target system by taking advantage of an unpatched vulnerability or weak configuration.

* `Installation` - The deployment of malware, backdoors, or persistence mechanisms on the compromised asset to maintain continuous access even after reboot.

* `Command & Control` - The establishment of a two-way communication channel (C2) between the infected host and the attacker's remote infrastructure to send commands and control the system.

* `Actions on Objectives` - The final stage where the adversary executes their ultimate goal, such as data exfiltration, system destruction, ransomware encryption, or lateral movement across the network.
